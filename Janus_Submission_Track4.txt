1. Functional MVP and Architecture Diagram
Overview: An Autonomous Financial Engine
There is a strategy that has been running quietly in the background of crypto for years: capturing funding rate spreads across perpetual exchanges. 

If Binance is paying 0.05% and Hyperliquid is paying 0.02%, institutional traders open opposing positions to capture the 0.03% difference with strictly zero directional risk. The problem? Executing this manually requires constant 24/7 API monitoring, instant execution, and massive capital. 

What the Janus Agent Does:
Janus automates the entire process, acting as a personal quantitative trading desk for everyday users. 
1. Scan: Every 15 minutes, the Janus agent pulls live funding rates from Binance, Bybit, KuCoin, MEXC, and Hyperliquid. Every asset listed on two or more exchanges gets evaluated autonomously.
2. Filter: Not every gap is worth acting on. The agent mathematically filters opportunities, ensuring transaction costs never eat into returns.
3. Execute: The agent identifies the best opportunity and acts on it, verifying vault balances via Circle Gateway and triggering the execution via our Arc smart contract.
4. Settle: The agent records the earnings directly on-chain, automatically compounding the yield back into the user's vault shares (jUSDC).

Example Real Execution by the Agent (Arc Testnet):
- Route: MEXC → KuCoin
- Capital deployed: $14,123.14 (testnet USDC)
- Spread captured: +0.5708%
- Earnings generated: $80.61 (testnet USDC)
- Transaction: 0xd16ddb…0277
The agent identified that two exchanges were pricing funding differently, took both positions, captured the spread, and recorded everything on-chain, entirely autonomously.

5. Circle Product Feedback
Why you chose these products for your use case:
We exclusively chose USDC and EURC on Arc as our settlement assets because executing market-neutral arbitrage requires a strictly fiat-denominated foundation. Otherwise, currency fluctuations would wipe out the yield margins. Circle Wallets provided a frictionless onboarding experience for retail users, and Gateway was critical in allowing our autonomous agent to efficiently route treasury funds for its arbitrage executions.

What worked well during development:
The predictability of USDC transactions on Arc made developing the logic for our autonomous agent incredibly straightforward. Settling in USDC ensures that when the agent completes a transaction, users see their APY in a stable, recognizable format. 

What could be improved:
Further integrations for bridging (CCTP) directly within the smart contract layer would allow our agent to autonomously onboard liquidity from other chains directly into the vault.

Recommendations to make the product or developer experience more seamless or scalable:
Providing more built-in, on-chain oracle feeds for standard institutional yields on Arc could further empower autonomous agents like the Janus Keeper to benchmark and route funds with even less reliance on off-chain calculation.
