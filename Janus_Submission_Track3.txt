1. Functional MVP and Architecture Diagram
Overview: The Strategy and The Gap
There is a strategy that has been running quietly in the background of crypto for years. It doesn't care if the market is going up or down. Janus runs a continuous scan on it, capturing a funding gap most traders never think about.

In crypto, perpetual futures use a "funding rate" to anchor the perp price to the spot price. Every 8 hours, one side of the market pays the other (longs pay shorts, or vice versa). Because every exchange has its own group of traders, the same asset can have completely different funding rates across different platforms.

Institutional traders figured this out years ago: Bet up on the exchange that pays you for betting up. Bet down on the exchange that pays you for betting down. Hold both at the same time. The price movement becomes irrelevant, and you collect the difference between the two rates every 8 hours. 

What Janus Does & How it is Protected:
Janus automates the entire process and puts it on-chain via Arc. Everything flows into two on-chain vaults (USDC and EURC). Users deposit stablecoins and receive vault shares (jUSDC). Every time the protocol executes an arbitrage, the total value in the vault increases. Your shares become worth more.

Because we are handling institutional capital, Janus is built with extreme protections:
1. Multiple Signers: Secured by a 5-of-9 multisig. No single person can change critical protocol settings.
2. Mandatory Timelocks: A cryptographically enforced 48-hour withdrawal queue. This prevents sudden mass exits and eliminates the risk of flash-loan sandwich manipulation on compounding yield.
3. Reserve Fund: The contract automatically takes a 5% performance fee on profitable trades and sends it directly to a transparent Insurance Fund to cover unexpected losses.
4. Full On-Chain Record: Every execution is permanently logged on-chain.

Architecture Diagram:
User -> Deposits USDC/EURC -> Janus Frontend App (Next.js)
App -> Connect Wallet & Transact -> JanusVault Smart Contract (Arc L1)
Vault -> Mints Yield-Bearing jUSDC/jEURC -> User
Keeper Bot -> Scans 5 Exchanges -> Filters Profitable Spreads
Keeper -> Executes Arbitrage -> Compounds Yield Back into Vault
Governance -> 5-of-9 Multisig -> 48-Hour Timelock -> Secures Vault
Vault -> 5% Performance Fee -> Insurance Fund Contract

5. Circle Product Feedback
Why you chose these products for your use case:
We exclusively chose USDC and EURC on Arc as our settlement assets because executing market-neutral arbitrage requires a strictly fiat-denominated foundation. Otherwise, currency fluctuations would wipe out the yield margins. Circle Wallets provided a frictionless onboarding experience for retail users, and Gateway was instrumental in efficiently routing treasury funds for the arbitrage executions.

What worked well during development:
The predictability of USDC transactions on Arc made developing the vault logic and calculating exact basis points for the spreads incredibly straightforward. Settling in USDC ensures our users see their APY in a stable, recognizable format. 

What could be improved:
Further integrations for bridging (CCTP) directly within the smart contract layer would make onboarding liquidity from other chains into the vault even more seamless.

Recommendations to make the product or developer experience more seamless or scalable:
Providing more built-in, on-chain oracle feeds for standard institutional yields on Arc could further empower automated protocols like Janus to benchmark and route funds more efficiently.
